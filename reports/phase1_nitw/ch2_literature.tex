\chapter{Literature Review}\label{ch:lit}

This chapter reviews the literature in five areas, from the clinical problem to the hardware: epilepsy and the need
for automatic detection (Section~\ref{sec:lit-clinical}), wearable and multimodal detection
(Section~\ref{sec:lit-wearable}), detection algorithms and their evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:lit-alg}), LUT-native,
weightless and logic networks (Section~\ref{sec:lit-lut}), and hardware seizure detectors
(Section~\ref{sec:lit-hw}). Section~\ref{sec:lit-gaps} summarises the research gaps. Every reference was checked
against a published record (title, authors, venue and year); numbers quoted from a paper are as reported in its
abstract unless stated otherwise.

\section{Epilepsy and the Need for Automatic Seizure Detection}\label{sec:lit-clinical}

Epilepsy affected an estimated 45.9 million people with active epilepsy in 2016 \cite{gbd2019epilepsy}. The ILAE 2017
operational classification \cite{fisher2017ilae} divides seizures by onset into focal and generalised; focal seizures
are further described by whether the patient stays aware (focal aware, focal impaired awareness), and a focal seizure
can spread into a focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizure. These are the classes annotated in SeizeIT2.

Patients are often unaware of their own seizures, which Elger and Hoppe identify as the main cause of seizure
under-reporting \cite{elger2018underreporting}; seizure diaries therefore undercount, and treatment decisions are made
on incomplete information. Seizures are also dangerous: in the MORTEMUS study of sudden unexpected death in epilepsy
(SUDEP) in monitoring units, the fatal cardiorespiratory dysfunction followed a generalised tonic-clonic seizure
\cite{ryvlin2013mortemus}. Objective, automatic seizure counting outside the hospital is therefore a clinical need.

The ILAE--IFCN clinical practice guideline \cite{beniczky2021guideline} gives a weak recommendation for wearable
devices that detect generalised and focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures, and does not recommend current devices
for other seizure types. Detecting \emph{focal} seizures, the majority in focal epilepsy, with a wearable remains open.

\section{Wearable and Multimodal Seizure Detection}\label{sec:lit-wearable}

\paragraph{Reviews and devices.} A review of multimodal wearable detection \cite{leijten2018review} found
sensitivities from 4\,\% to 100\,\% across studies, with tonic-clonic seizures detected best. A wearable EMG device
detected tonic-clonic seizures with more than 90\,\% sensitivity in a 71-patient study \cite{beniczky2018emg}, and
wrist-worn electrodermal-activity and accelerometer sensors reached 94.55\,\% sensitivity at 0.2 false alarms per day
for convulsive seizures \cite{onorati2017empatica}. A meta-analysis \cite{naganur2022meta} pooled a sensitivity of 0.91
for tonic-clonic seizures at 2.1 false alarms per 24 hours. All of this evidence concerns convulsive seizures.

\paragraph{Behind-the-ear EEG and the SeizeIT project.} EEG is the most direct signal of a seizure, but a full scalp
EEG is not wearable for days. The SeizeIT project records EEG from electrodes behind the ears. Vandecasteele et al.
\cite{vandecasteele2020bte} showed that seizures can be annotated and detected on behind-the-ear EEG, with a
patient-specific detector at 69.1\,\% sensitivity and 0.49 false positives per 24\,h and a patient-independent one at
64.1\,\% and 2.8 per 24\,h. Adding ECG to a two-channel EEG detector raised sensitivity by 8--11\,\% at the same
false-alarm rate \cite{vandecasteele2021ecg}, and helped human reviewers too: with ECG, false detections fell from 6.5
to 2.4 per day \cite{bhagubai2023ecg}. The ICASSP 2023 Grand Challenge on wearable-EEG seizure detection
\cite{bhagubai2024challenge} was won by an ensemble of decision trees. SeizeIT2 \cite{bhagubai2025seizeit2} then
released 125 patients with more than 11,000 hours of behind-the-ear EEG, ECG, EMG and motion, with ChronoNet and SVM
baselines; it is the dataset of this project. A multicentre clinical validation of the same wearable on 192 adults
\cite{swinnen2026seizeit2} reached an algorithmic sensitivity of 0.73 at a precision of only 0.004 before human review,
which shows that false alarms are the main obstacle for focal seizures.

\paragraph{Other work on SeizeIT2.} A study of tonic-clonic seizures in the SeizeIT2 cohort \cite{zhang2024tcs} fused
EEG, EMG and accelerometry and reached 90.9\,\% sensitivity at 0.1 false positives per 24\,h, against 100\,\% at 10.3
per 24\,h for EEG alone: fusion mainly removes false alarms. ECG-only anomaly detection \cite{reintjes2025ecg} and a
complex-valued multimodal network \cite{su2026cmepinet} have also been applied to SeizeIT2; none of these reports a
hardware budget.

\paragraph{Fusion and missing modalities.} BrainFuseNet \cite{ingolfsson2024brainfusenet} fused EEG, PPG and
accelerometry on a microcontroller (64.28\,\% sensitivity at 0.21 false positives per hour). A cyclic transformer
trained on EEG and EMG but tested on EEG alone \cite{zhang2024cyclic} is the only work we found that targets a missing
modality at test time. Table~\ref{tab:lit-wearable} summarises this section.

\section{Seizure Detection Algorithms and Their Evaluation}\label{sec:lit-alg}

\paragraph{Feature-based detectors.} Shoeb and Guttag \cite{shoeb2010icml} trained a patient-specific support vector
machine on EEG spectral features and detected 96\,\% of 173 test seizures on the CHB-MIT scalp EEG database, with a
median latency of 3\,s and 2 false detections per 24\,h. Patient-specific training needs seizures of the same patient,
which a wearable for new patients does not have. On wearable behind-the-ear EEG, the winner of the ICASSP 2023 Grand
Challenge, SeizFt \cite{alhussaini2023seizft}, used hand-crafted features, Fourier-transform-surrogate augmentation
and a gradient-boosted tree ensemble (CatBoost); a random forest on band-power features reached 73.6\,\% sensitivity
and 96.7\,\% specificity in the same challenge \cite{huang2023lightweight}. Tree ensembles are the strongest published
approach on SeizeIT data, which is why LightGBM, XGBoost and CatBoost are our software references.

\paragraph{Deep-learning detectors.} ChronoNet \cite{roy2019chrononet} stacks multi-scale one-dimensional convolutions
and densely connected GRU layers on raw EEG; it was designed for abnormal-EEG classification and is the deep baseline
of SeizeIT2. In the ICASSP 2023 challenge, ChronoNet trained on SeizeIT1 reached only 15.2\,\% sensitivity on the
SeizeIT2 validation patients \cite{huang2023lightweight}. EEGNet \cite{lawhern2018eegnet} is a compact convolutional
network for EEG; EEGWaveNet \cite{thuwajit2022eegwavenet} extracts multiscale features with trainable depthwise
convolutions and improves subject-independent detection; graph neural networks with self-supervised pre-training
\cite{tang2022gnnssl} reached a detection AUROC of 0.875 on TUSZ but need a full electrode montage to build the graph;
U-Net transformers \cite{wu2025seizuretransformer} and multi-signal transformers such as BIOT \cite{yang2023biot} push
accuracy further with much larger models. These networks are floating-point and multiplier-based; their size is
reported in parameters, not in hardware cost.

\paragraph{Learning from imperfect labels.} Seizure onset and offset are uncertain even for experts. Weak supervision
with noisy clinical labels \cite{saab2020weak}, attention-based multiple-instance learning when only time periods are
labelled \cite{isaev2020mil}, and uncertainty-aware training with noisy labels \cite{shama2026bundl} address this on
scalp EEG. Knowledge distillation from a full-montage teacher into a two-channel student \cite{zheng2023kdgnn} keeps
detection competitive with very few channels. These ideas motivate our extensions N4 (distillation) and N5
(noise-robust and multiple-instance losses), applied here to a LUT network.

\paragraph{Datasets and evaluation.} Most detectors are developed on scalp EEG: CHB-MIT and the TUH Seizure Corpus
\cite{shah2018tusz}. How a detector is scored changes its numbers: epoch-based scoring, any-overlap event scoring and
time-aligned event scoring give different results for the same system \cite{ziyabari2017metrics}. SzCORE
\cite{dan2024szcore} standardised data formats, cross-validation and event-based metrics (sensitivity, precision, F1,
false positives per day) with an open-source scorer. In the first blind SzCORE challenge
\cite{dan2025szcorechallenge}, on 65 unseen subjects and 4,360 hours of EEG, the best algorithm reached an event-F1 of
43\,\% (sensitivity 37\,\%, precision 45\,\%), and reported results did not hold up on blind data. Data leakage,
letting a subject's data appear in both training and test sets, is common in EEG deep learning and inflates results
\cite{brookshire2024leakage}. Table~\ref{tab:lit-alg} summarises this section.

\section{LUT-Native, Weightless and Logic Networks}\label{sec:lit-lut}

\paragraph{Weightless neural networks.} A weightless network replaces weighted sums with memory lookups. WiSARD
\cite{aleksander1984wisard} split a binary input into small tuples, each addressing a RAM node, with one discriminator
per class whose score is the number of RAM nodes that fire. Such networks are trained by writing tables, not by
gradient descent, and their inputs must already be binary. BTHOWeN \cite{susskind2022bthowen} added Bloom filters,
hashing and a Gaussian thermometer encoding of continuous inputs, with an FPGA accelerator; ULEEN
\cite{susskind2023uleen} borrowed training techniques from binarised networks and reported 3.6 times the
area-normalised throughput and 7.1 times the energy efficiency of a FINN binarised network on FPGA.

\paragraph{Differentiable Weightless Neural Networks.} The DWN \cite{bacellar2024dwn} made the lookup tables
themselves trainable end to end: thermometer-encoded inputs, LUT layers trained with an Extended Finite Difference
gradient, and a learnable input mapping that decides which bits each LUT reads. The authors report better latency,
throughput, energy and area than earlier FPGA designs, and better accuracy than XGBoost on a microcontroller under
tight memory limits. DWNs have since been applied to wearable inertial activity recognition \cite{bacellar2025nanoml}
(96.3--96.7\,\% accuracy at 56--104\,nJ per sample) and to continuous control with thermometer-encoded sensor inputs
compiled to FPGA logic \cite{kresse2025dwc}. The DWN is the base model of this project.

\paragraph{Logic-gate networks.} Deep Differentiable Logic Gate Networks \cite{petersen2022dlgn} learn, for every
neuron, which of the 16 two-input Boolean functions to compute, and after training run as pure logic (over a million
MNIST images per second on one CPU core). Convolutional LGNs \cite{petersen2024convdlgn} reach about 86\,\% on
CIFAR-10 with tens of millions of gates; Light LGNs \cite{ruttgers2026lightdlgn} reduce the parameters of
higher-fan-in gates; a recurrent LGN \cite{buhrer2025rdlgn} is the only stateful logic network we found, evaluated on
machine translation.

\paragraph{LUT-based networks for FPGAs.} LUTNet \cite{wang2019lutnet} replaced the XNOR operations of binarised
networks with trainable K-input LUTs. LogicNets \cite{umuroglu2020logicnets} trains sparse networks with few-bit
activations so that every neuron can be enumerated as a truth table and synthesised directly into FPGA logic, with
latencies below 15\,ns; its cost grows exponentially with fan-in, which keeps fan-in tiny. PolyLUT
\cite{andronic2023polylut} lets each LUT neuron compute a polynomial of its inputs, and NeuraLUT
\cite{andronic2024neuralut} hides a small dense sub-network inside each LUT (up to 4.3 times lower latency at equal
accuracy). AmigoLUT \cite{weng2025amigolut} scales LUT networks through ensembles, and ReducedLUT
\cite{cassidy2025reducedlut} compresses the tables by treating input patterns never seen in training as ``don't care''
(up to 1.63 times fewer LUTs), an assumption that is risky when a new patient produces patterns the training patients
did not. TreeLUT \cite{khataei2025treelut} maps quantised gradient-boosted trees into LUT-only logic with 4--101 times
lower area-delay product than recent LUT networks; we use it as a strong non-neural LUT baseline.

\paragraph{The cost of the encoder.} Mecik and Kumm \cite{mecik2025thermo} built a complete DWN hardware generator and
found that the thermometer encoder can raise LUT usage by up to 3.2 times and dominates the cost of small networks.
Our own cost estimate agrees: in our 50-LUT base model the encoder accounts for about 850 of about 960 LUTs
(Chapter~\ref{ch:method}), which is why encoder design (N2) is one of our extensions.

\paragraph{LUT and logic networks on biosignals.} Mommen et al. \cite{mommen2026ecg} classified ECG arrhythmias
across patients with LGNs and LUT networks, up to 94.3\,\% accuracy on MIT-BIH with 2,000--2,990 LUTs on an Artix-7
FPGA (about 5--7\,mW, estimated). Dharia et al. \cite{dharia2026eeglgn} applied LGNs to EEG classification (dementia
and emotion) on an embedded CPU. The only weightless network applied to seizures is a WiSARD model for seizure
anticipation evaluated on 4 patients, with accuracy as the only metric \cite{deaguiar2015wisard}.

\section{Hardware Seizure Detectors and Efficient Inference}\label{sec:lit-hw}

\paragraph{ASICs.} The first micro-power seizure-detection chips put the analogue front end and feature extraction on
chip: Verma et al. \cite{verma2010micro} extracted spectral EEG features at 9\,\textmu J per feature vector and sent them
to a central classifier, which cut system power 14 times by avoiding raw-EEG transmission. Later systems-on-chip ran
the classifier too: a 16-channel patient-specific SVM chip at 2.73\,\textmu J per classification \cite{altaf2015jssc},
a non-linear SVM chip at 1.83\,\textmu J per classification \cite{altaf2016tbcas}, and an SVM processor with on-chip
learning at 1.9\,mW \cite{huang2020jssc}. Tree-based classifiers proved particularly cheap: gradient-boosted trees on
intracranial EEG at 41.2\,nJ per classification \cite{shoaran2018jetcas}, and the NeuralTree chip at
0.227\,\textmu J per classification across 256 channels \cite{shin2022neuraltree}. Trees only compare and add, which is
also why they map well to lookup tables.

\paragraph{FPGAs.} FPGA detectors include an STFT and SVM design on a Zynq-7000 at 380\,mW of programmable-logic power
\cite{wang2018access}, SVM training and classification on a Virtex-7 \cite{elhosary2019tbcas}, a patient-specific
intracranial-EEG detector on a Cyclone~V at 0.49\,mW dynamic power \cite{razi2022tbcas}, an ultra-low-complexity
line-length detector \cite{vittimberga2025sensors}, and a spiking neural network on a small Lattice iCE40UP5K
\cite{busia2025fpga}, which reported 4.55\,nJ and 0.5\,\textmu s per inference with 0.3 false positives per hour on
CHB-MIT using four temporal EEG channels. The last is the closest to our Phase II target: a low-cost FPGA and few
channels near the ear.

\paragraph{Microcontrollers.} e-Glass \cite{sopic2018eglass} put four EEG electrodes in a glasses frame and ran
detection on an embedded processor for 2.71 days per charge. EEGformer \cite{busia2024eegformer} ran a compact
transformer on temporal EEG channels at 0.31\,mJ per inference and detected 73\,\% of CHB-MIT seizures at 0.15 false
positives per hour. BrainFuseNet \cite{ingolfsson2024brainfusenet} fused EEG, PPG and accelerometry on the same class
of microcontroller at 0.11\,mJ per inference. Tree-based EEG artefact detection on a low-power processor
\cite{ingolfsson2022artifact} and the BioWolf platform \cite{kartsch2019biowolf} show the same push towards wearable,
always-on processing.

\paragraph{Tools for efficient inference.} Binarised neural networks \cite{hubara2016bnn} reduce weights and
activations to one bit; FINN \cite{umuroglu2017finn} compiles them into streaming FPGA dataflow; integer-only
quantisation \cite{jacob2018quant} made 8-bit inference standard; hls4ml \cite{duarte2018hls4ml} translates trained
networks into FPGA firmware with latencies around 100\,ns. Quantised networks still need integer multipliers (FPGA DSP
blocks); our INT8 MLP baseline represents this approach. Table~\ref{tab:lit-hw} summarises the hardware detectors.
