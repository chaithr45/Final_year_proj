\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\markboth{Abstract}{}

Automatic seizure detection with wearable sensors could give people with epilepsy objective seizure counts and timely
alarms, but the detector has to run for days on a small battery, next to the sensors. In this project we ask whether
a \emph{LUT-native} neural network, whose neurons are the six-input lookup tables (LUTs) that make up an FPGA, can
detect seizures from behind-the-ear EEG, ECG, EMG and motion signals in patients it has never seen, with no
multipliers in the classifier and a budget of a few thousand LUTs.

We use SeizeIT2, a public wearable dataset of 125 patients with focal epilepsy (11,626 hours of recordings, 883
annotated seizures), with its official split into 96 training and 29 test patients. In Phase I, over the first two
months of the project, we audited every file of the dataset and found that it differs from its documentation (all
biopotentials are sampled at 256~Hz instead of 250~Hz, and the files hold 883 seizures instead of 886). We reproduced
the published ChronoNet baseline: the published sensitivity was not reached (74.5\,\% against 84.2\,\%) at far fewer
false alarms, and we found that the released code selects its model on the test patients, so we also trained a clean
version that uses no test information. We designed a bit-exact integer feature pipeline of 117 features from the four
modalities, with a fixed-point specification; it loses no accuracy against floating point (AUROC 0.736 against
0.735). We then compared nine model families with patient-wise five-fold cross-validation on the training patients
and event-based SzCORE metrics.

On cross-validation, a Differentiable Weightless Neural Network of 50 six-input LUTs reaches an event-F1 of 0.211
with 2.4 false alarms per day, at an estimated 960 LUTs and no multipliers. It is seven to eight times smaller than
the next LUT-based models (TreeLUT and a logic-gate network) and better on every metric than an INT8 neural network
that needs 9,600 multiplications per decision, but it is still below gradient-boosted trees (event-F1 0.292). It
detects 88\,\% of tonic-clonic seizures but few focal seizures, and it depends on the ECG signal. An interpretability
study of the network showed that the first feature selection ignored the heart-rate features; selecting the inputs by
LightGBM importance instead raised event-F1 from 0.148 to 0.208 at the same size, and training with random sensor
dropout keeps the detector working when the ECG is missing (event-F1 0.172 against 0.092). The literature survey of 79
references found no LUT-native seizure detector and no LUT network that fuses modalities or handles missing sensors.

Distillation from LightGBM was adopted as the final network design. With the design frozen, the models were evaluated
once on the 29 held-out test patients with five seeds: the final network reached an event-F1 of 0.248 with 0.8 false
alarms per day at about 900 LUTs, above the clean ChronoNet baseline (0.027 with 106 false alarms per day; per-patient
p = 0.027) and TreeLUT (0.233), level with random forests (0.246) and below LightGBM (0.308). Its generated Verilog
matches the software on all 431,960 windows of 10 test recordings. Phase II will implement and measure the detector on
an FPGA.
